# Unli Rice 1.5 (Mac) — 6-Month Post-Mortem, run as a premortem (2026-10-31)

The gate in `_AI Context/07_Prelaunch_Post_Mortem.md`, run before Submit for Review. Written as
if 1.5 shipped in November 2026 and failed by May 2027, then working back. The founder asked to
see it alongside the 1.5 (10) upload (2026-10-31).

## Context & data

1. **The product.** 1.5 replaces the 1.4 sidebar with two tabs. **Studio** shows your projects
   as a stack of cards: an icon and a sticker (FINE, NEEDS YOU, NO NOTES YET, CAN'T READ), seven
   lights from Idea to Post-mortem, a Next step with Copy prompt, the project's branches and its
   notes. **Guardrails & Grounding** holds what 1.4 had: notes, the folder your AI reads, House
   Rules, Need you, safety checks. Setup, Trust Center and the extras moved to a gear menu. One
   bug fixed (the House Rules templates page closed the window).
2. **Audience and value.** People who run several AI coding assistants on several projects and
   want one place to see where each project stands and what the assistants know.
3. **Pricing.** Free, open source, MIT. No account, no in-app purchase.
4. **The outcome (imagined).** A user updates, opens Studio, picks their Projects folder, and
   sees six dotted circles on every card ("not recorded") and Next: Idea. Their repos don't have
   `docs/PLAN-*.md` files with a `Stage:` line, approval headers or `docs/dialogue/` reviews, so the
   main new screen tells them nothing. The hero screenshot says NEEDS YOU on the card and
   "0 need you" beside it. 1.4 users who had just learned the sidebar get a different layout a
   week later. Reviews: "pretty, but what are the lights for?" Downloads stay flat.
5. **Assumptions.** That other developers plan the way this studio does (intent → spec → plan →
   pre-mortem → build → post-mortem, written as files in `docs/`); that a project dashboard brings
   people back to a memory app; that a second redesign within a week reads as progress.
   **Unknown:** real usage. The 1.4 premortem asked for real counts before cutting further; none
   were taken.

## 1. The core paradox

Studio is a mirror of the founder's own pipeline. The lights read approval headers, `@AGENTS.md`
imports, `PLAN-*.md` stages and Codex/Claude dialogue turns (`BuildGate.swift:21–104`). For anyone
who doesn't write those files, a repo with no plan shows six "not recorded" lights
(`BuildGate.swift:66`). People say they want to see where their projects stand, but they won't
adopt a document process to make a dashboard light up. The parts that work for everyone (cards,
icons, branches, notes per project) are the quieter half of the screen.

## 2. Distribution & friction

- **First run.** A fresh install opens on the connect screen, then on an empty Studio that asks
  for a folder. The value only appears after granting a folder, and then, for most repos, the
  lights are blank.
- **Two layouts in one week.** 1.4 went live on 2026-10-31 with a Grounding/Guardrails sidebar;
  1.5 replaces it. What's New explains it, but 1.4 users relearn twice.
- **The listing contradicts itself.** Screenshot 01 and the website hero show NEEDS YOU on
  recipe-box and "0 need you" in its Notes deck. The sticker counts to-dos and flags
  (`ProjectGarden.swift:36`); the Notes deck counts only pending flags on that project's notes
  (`NotesDeck.swift:15–18`). A careful viewer sees a bug in the first image.
- **Time to value for the memory side** is unchanged: connecting an assistant still means
  pasting a config block into another app's file.

## 3. The paywall / conversion trap

No price, so the cost is attention and trust: granting folders, pasting configs, learning a new
layout. The conversion that fails is the second open. If Studio shows nothing useful on day one,
there is no reason to come back to it, and the Guardrails & Grounding tab is the same as 1.4.

## 4. Blind spots & flawed assumptions

- **"Plan high, build low" is not a market.** The lights encode one studio's process. Without an
  explanation in the app of what files turn a light green, users can't make them work even if
  they want to. The new caption ("Lights read this repo's plan, approvals and reviews.") is
  accurate but doesn't say how.
- **Two counts, one word.** "Need you" means different things on the card and in the deck.
- **Copy prompt is the bridge and it's hidden.** It handles a repo with no plan
  (`SleeveBackView.swift:99–105`), but it only appears in the expanded view.
- **Usage is still unmeasured.** Third release in a row without real counts.

## 5. Key takeaways & anti-patterns

1. **A new screen must say something on an ordinary repo.** For a repo with no plan, show what
   the app can read (branches, notes, last activity) above the lights, and put "Copy prompt to
   start a plan" on the front card, not only in the expanded view.
2. **One word, one count.** Make the Notes deck's "need you" match the sticker (to-dos plus
   flags), or rename one of them. Then re-shoot screenshot 01 and the website hero.
3. **Explain the lights where they are.** A short "How lights turn green" link from the caption
   to the user guide's lights section.
4. **Stop reorganising, start measuring.** No further layout changes until the installed app's
   real counts (projects scanned, notes written per assistant) are looked at.
5. **Say who Studio is for.** In the listing and on the site: "for people who plan their AI
   work in files", rather than implying it works on any repo.

## Verdict

Shippable as is: nothing in 1.5 is broken or misleading enough to fail review, and the review
steps were walked on an empty vault. **Takeaway 2 is the one I'd fix before submitting**: it's a
one-line count change in `NotesDeck.swift`, then a new archive (still build 10 if it hasn't been
uploaded yet, otherwise 11), and screenshot 01 plus the website hero re-shot. Takeaways 1 and 3 are 1.6 work.
Takeaways 4 and 5 don't block 1.5.
